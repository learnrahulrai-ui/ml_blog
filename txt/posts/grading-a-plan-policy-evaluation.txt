  PATH . SPECIAL -- Policy Evaluation From Zero  (standalone; no other page on this blog needed)

  Every reinforcement-learning post hands you the Bellman equation -- V(s) = R(s) + g * V(next)
  -- names it after a dead mathematician, and says "now just iterate until it converges." Two
  things that post always skips. First: WHY starting every value at 0 -- a flat lie -- ever
  crawls to the right answer. That smells like astrology: begin wrong, end right, no reason
  given. Second: WHY the discount g (the 0.9) is not a "care less about tomorrow" mood knob but
  the one thing that forces a finite answer to exist at all. This page shows both, digit by
  digit, with a pencil. The formula appears at the end, after you have already used it.

  THE RULE OF THIS PAGE. Assume you remember nothing. Every word is defined where it first
  appears. Anything named more than five lines ago is named again. Every number is worked in
  front of you, never "recall that...". One idea per line. A pencil and this page are the whole
  kit. Decimals shown to four places where they matter.

  TOY WORLD, REAL RECIPE. One line of honesty up front. The RECIPE -- start at a lie, lay the
  same two-line rule down again and again, watch it settle -- is exact. The WORLD is a toy:
  a tiny stack of spots, simple painted scores, a plan with one fixed arrow per spot. Real
  policy evaluation lets the arrow land on several next-spots by chance and adds them up
  weighted by their odds; here the plan is fixed and the arrow lands on exactly ONE next-spot,
  so that weighted sum collapses to a single term. That collapse is the toy. It is named again,
  loudly, in the box at the end -- so the one place we hide a sum behind a single arrow is never
  smuggled past you. Trust the recipe; treat the rest as a sketchpad.

  The one move, named here so you are not surprised later:

      A worth leans on its neighbour's worth, which leans on ITS neighbour's worth -- a circle.
      You cannot write a bare number for any spot up front. So you LIE: write 0 everywhere,
      then lay the rule down over the table again and again. The lie heals. The truth climbs
      out from one painted anchor, one spot per pass, until nothing moves.

  That single trick turns a circular formula into plain arithmetic you can check by hand.


  ## The World (the picture everything lives in)

  A drone hangs in the air over a patch of enemy ground. We want to grade a flight PLAN.

      a SPOT      = a place the drone can be: a position with a height.
      the GROUND  = the lowest spots. Touching the ground ENDS the flight.
      the RUNWAY  = one special ground spot. Touch it -> +5 points. (the prize)
      DIRT        = any other ground spot. Touch it -> -2 points. (a crash)
      the AIR     = any spot above the ground. The drone does not stop here; it must move on.
      FUEL        = sitting in the air for one move costs -0.1 points. (the meter always runs)
      the ARROW   = the PLAN. On every air spot, an arrow says "from here, go THIS way."
      the SHRINK  = a worth one move into the future counts as 0.9 of itself. (the famous "g")

  These are PAINTED FACTS. +5, -2, -0.1, 0.9 -- handed to you, fixed, never argued with.

  Jargon parked here, to be ignored until the decoder at the end: a spot = a "state". The plan
  = the "policy". A spot's worth = its "value" V(s). Painted points = the "reward". The shrink
  = the "discount factor", written gamma. Touching the ground = a "terminal state".


  ## What "Grade the Plan" Even Means -- the WORTH of a Spot

  Someone hands you the PLAN (the arrows). You are NOT asked to improve it. You are asked one
  thing, for every spot:

      if the drone starts on this spot and obeys the arrows to the very end,
      how many points does it walk away with?

  That number is the spot's WORTH. Fill in the worth of every spot and you have graded the
  plan -- a good plan scores high worths, a bad plan scores low ones. That is the whole job.
  Nothing here invents arrows or picks a better path. It only SCORES the arrows you were given.


  ## The One Law (derived, not declared)

  A spot's worth = the points the drone collects from here on, obeying the arrows. Where the
  spot sits decides the shape.

  **A ground spot.** Touching the ground ends the flight. Nothing comes after. There is no
  future to count -- the worth is just the points painted there:

      worth(ground)  =  its painted points          (+5 on the runway, -2 on dirt)

  No lookup, no neighbour, no arithmetic. The number is painted on. Burn that in: a ground
  spot's worth is TRUE the instant you write it down, and can never be wrong.

  **An air spot.** The drone does not stop; it pays fuel and rides the arrow onward. Two parts:

      it pays fuel right now ........................... -0.1
      one move along the arrow sits the NEXT spot,
      whose worth counts 0.9 of itself (the shrink) ... 0.9 * worth(next)

  Add the two:

      worth(air)  =  -0.1  +  0.9 * worth(next)

  That is the entire law. Two lines. (`next` = the single spot the arrow points to.)


  ## Why There Is No One-Shot Formula -- the Circle

  Look hard at the air line:

      worth(air)  =  -0.1  +  0.9 * worth(next)

  To get this spot's worth you need `worth(next)`. But if `next` is also an air spot, ITS worth
  needs the worth of ITS next, and on it goes. A worth leans on another worth, which leans on
  another. You cannot just write `worth = <number>` for an air spot -- the number you need is
  one you do not have yet. That circle is the whole obstacle. Your gut is right to balk: there
  is no formula that spits out the answer in one shot.


  ## Breaking the Circle -- the WALK, and Starting From a Lie

  New word, defined by a picture:

      A WALK = run your finger down the list of spots, top to bottom, ONE time, writing each
               spot's worth from the law above -- using whatever the table holds RIGHT NOW,
               even if that neighbour's worth is still a lie.

  (Picture a shopping list: one finger-trip down it = one walk. Down it again = a second walk.)

  The recipe in full:

      set every spot's worth to 0          <- a deliberate lie, just to have a starting table
      do a walk. do another. do another.
      stop when a walk moves nothing.

  That is it. There is NO line that checks "is the neighbour ready," no junk-detector, no
  convergence test beyond "did anything move." Just the two-line law, laid down again and again.


  ## Why Starting From a Lie Still Lands on the Truth (the heart, with numbers)

  Two facts make the lie heal. Neither is magic; both are arithmetic.

  **The "+" lays down a SOLID layer.** The air law is `reward + 0.9 * next`. The reward part
  (the -0.1 fuel) is solid -- it is painted, it needs no lookup, it goes down on every walk no
  matter what the neighbour says. So even reading a junk neighbour (still 0):

      worth  =  -0.1  +  0.9 * 0  =  -0.1          <- NOT 0. A solid -0.1 got laid down.

  The next walk adds another -0.1 plus 0.9 times a now-firmer neighbour. The value BUILDS by
  addition. Junk cannot multiply into junk-forever, because a solid layer is added on top every
  pass. The "+" is the floor under the "*".

  **The ground is a fixed ANCHOR.** A ground spot's worth is its painted points, with no
  lookup -- correct from the first walk, never junk. That is the one fixed truth every air spot
  is dragged toward.

  Watch it heal. Three spots stacked straight above the runway, arrows pointing DOWN, finger
  running top to bottom (T first, then M, then G) -- the order that FORCES the stale read you
  were worried about, because T and M get written before the spot they lean on.

      T = top    (air),  arrow down -> M
      M = middle (air),  arrow down -> G
      G = ground = runway,  painted +5     (the anchor)

  Everything starts at 0.

      WALK 1   (start T=0, M=0, G=0)
        T:  -> M reads 0 (stale!)   worth = -0.1 + 0.9*0  = -0.1
        M:  -> G reads 0 (stale!)   worth = -0.1 + 0.9*0  = -0.1
        G:  ground -> +5                                          <- anchor lands. TRUE forever.
        table: T=-0.1   M=-0.1   G=+5

      WALK 2   (start T=-0.1, M=-0.1, G=+5)
        T:  -> M reads -0.1          worth = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.1) = -0.19
        M:  -> G reads +5 (true!)    worth = -0.1 + 0.9*5      =  4.4   <- M is now TRUE
        G:  +5
        table: T=-0.19   M=4.4   G=+5

      WALK 3   (start T=-0.19, M=4.4, G=+5)
        T:  -> M reads 4.4 (true!)   worth = -0.1 + 0.9*4.4 = 3.86      <- T is now TRUE
        M:  -> G reads +5            worth = 4.4   (no change)
        G:  +5
        table: T=3.86   M=4.4   G=+5

      WALK 4   nothing moves. 3.86, 4.4, +5 stay put. STOP.

  Follow the top spot heal:

      T:   0   ->   -0.1   ->   -0.19   ->   3.86   ->   3.86
          start    walk1      walk2       walk3      walk4 (frozen)

  It started as junk read off a stale 0 and it healed. The reason, with no hand-waving:

      G (the anchor) is correct from walk 1.
      M leans on G  -> M becomes correct one walk later (walk 2).
      T leans on M  -> T becomes correct one walk after that (walk 3).

  The truth climbs ONE spot per walk, out from the anchor. The painted +5 cannot lie, and the
  rule drags the next spot to it, then the next. As tall as the stack is, that is how many walks
  the truth needs to reach the top. Then it freezes.

  > We do not start from a lie and hope. We start from a lie sitting next to ONE painted truth,
  > and plain multiplication drags every spot to that truth, one spot closer per pass.


  ## The Harder Case: a Forever-Bounce With NO Ground at All

  "Fine," you say, "it heals because there is a +5 anchor to climb to. What if the drone never
  lands?" Good -- that is the case that looks impossible. Two air spots, A and B, arrows
  pointing at each OTHER forever. The drone bounces A, B, A, B... and never touches ground.
  No anchor anywhere. Both start at 0.

      WALK 1   A = -0.1 + 0.9*(0)        = -0.1
               B = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.1)     = -0.19
      WALK 2   A = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.19)    = -0.271
               B = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.271)   = -0.3439
      WALK 3   A = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.3439)  = -0.40951
        ...    -0.1 -> -0.271 -> -0.41 -> -0.52 -> ... -> -1

  It crawls to -1 and holds. No ground was ever touched, yet it settled on a real number. How
  can an endless bounce cost a finite amount? Because of the shrink.


  ## Why the Shrink Forces a Finite Answer (the math cracked from scratch)

  Bounce forever, paying -0.1 a move, with one more shrink stacked on each move:

      total  =  -0.1  +  0.9*(-0.1)  +  0.9^2*(-0.1)  +  0.9^3*(-0.1)  +  ...
             =  -0.1 * ( 1 + 0.9 + 0.9^2 + 0.9^3 + ... )

  Call the bracket S. Derive S from nothing -- no formula recalled, built right here:

      S        =  1 + 0.9 + 0.9^2 + 0.9^3 + ...
      0.9 * S  =      0.9 + 0.9^2 + 0.9^3 + ...
      S - 0.9S =  1                          (every later term cancels, one against the next)
      S(1 - 0.9) = 1
      S = 1 / (1 - 0.9) = 1 / 0.1 = 10

  So total = -0.1 * 10 = -1. Infinite moves, FINITE total -- the far terms shrink to nothing.
  Cross-check by the circle itself: with A = B = x, the law says x = -0.1 + 0.9x, so 0.1x = -0.1,
  so x = -1. Two routes, same -1.

  Now the punchline. Drop the shrink -- use a plain 1 instead of 0.9. The bracket becomes
  1 + 1 + 1 + ... = infinity. The worth is -infinity. The walks NEVER settle -- THAT would be
  the junk-forever you feared. So the 0.9 < 1 is not a mood ("value the future a little less").
  It is the floor that forbids an infinite bounce from summing to nothing-finite. The shrink is
  what makes an answer EXIST. That is its real job, and almost no tutorial says so.


  ## When to Stop -- delta and theta

  Two more words, defined:

      delta = the biggest move any spot made on a walk = the largest (new minus old), sign dropped.
      theta = a tiny line, e.g. 0.00000001 -- "near enough."

      stop rule:  when delta < theta, nothing moved more than a hair -> the table obeys the law
                  everywhere -> the worths have settled -> STOP.

  A safety cap on the number of walks is just a seatbelt so a bug cannot loop forever. The real
  stop is delta < theta.


  ## There Are NO Special Checks -- the Settling Is EMERGENT

  This is the part that feels too small to work. There is no code that spots junk, waits for a
  neighbour, or measures "convergence." The whole engine is the two-line law, laid down again
  and again, with the delta-vs-theta stop. The healing above and the finite bounce are
  CONSEQUENCES of repeating a "+ and *0.9" rule -- they are written nowhere. The code looks too
  simple to deserve the result, and that is correct: the work lives in the REPETITION and the
  0.9, not in any clever check.


  ## A Full Table, Filled by Hand -- and What It Reveals

  One more pass, the whole picture, so the payoff is concrete. A 3-wide, 3-tall world: spots
  are (x, y, height), each of x, y, height in {0,1,2}. The runway is the middle ground spot
  (1,1,0) -> +5. Every other ground spot is dirt -> -2. The plan: always nudge DOWN. So an air
  spot (x, y, height) leans on the spot one below it, (x, y, height-1). Walk each stack bottom
  to top, so the spot below is already written this same walk.

  The stack ABOVE the runway -- sitting (1,1,0)=+5, (1,1,1), (1,1,2):

      (1,1,0)  ground, the runway  -> +5
      (1,1,1)  -0.1 + 0.9*( +5 )   =  4.4
      (1,1,2)  -0.1 + 0.9*( 4.4 )  =  3.86

  ANY OTHER stack -- sitting on dirt (?, ?, 0) = -2:

      (x,y,0)  ground, dirt        -> -2
      (x,y,1)  -0.1 + 0.9*( -2 )   = -1.9
      (x,y,2)  -0.1 + 0.9*( -1.9 ) = -1.81

  The finished table, read at a glance:

      runway stack (1,1,*):     +5 ,  4.4 ,  3.86      <- sitting over the runway pays off
      every other stack:        -2 , -1.9 , -1.81      <- this plan dives straight into dirt

  The grade is in the numbers. "Always Down" is brilliant in exactly ONE stack -- the column
  over the runway -- and a disaster in the other eight, because everywhere else, Down means
  crash into bare dirt (-2). That worth table is the scorecard a NEXT step would read to re-aim
  the arrows (head toward the runway stack first, THEN drop) -- but re-aiming is a different
  job. Grading the plan we were handed is done.


  ## The Decoder (jargon -> plain, master sheet)

      THE PEACOCK WORD                         WHAT IT ACTUALLY MEANS
      --------------------------------------   -------------------------------------------------
      state / s                                a spot -- a place the drone can be
      policy / pi                              the plan -- one arrow per spot, handed to you, fixed
      value / V(s)                             the spot's worth -- points collected from here on
      reward / R(s)                            the painted points at a spot (+5 / -2 / -0.1)
      terminal state                           a ground spot -- touching it ends the flight
      discount factor / gamma                  the shrink (0.9) -- a future worth counts 0.9 per move
      transition / result(s, a)                where the arrow lands you next
      Bellman equation                         the one law: worth = reward + shrink * worth(next)
      policy evaluation                        the whole job: fill every spot's worth for a fixed plan
      iterative policy evaluation              do it by walks -- start at 0, lay the law down, repeat
      sweep / iteration                        one walk down the list of spots
      convergence                              the walks stopped moving -- delta fell below theta
      delta                                    biggest worth-move on a walk
      theta                                    the "near enough" line that ends the walks
      bootstrapping (RL sense)                 a worth leaning on another (still-settling) worth


  ## Python: the Settling, by Hand, Three Ways

  Three blocks. The first two are hard-coded spot by spot -- no loops hiding the decisions, so
  you can check every digit against the walks above. The third is the real engine; the one
  honest place a loop earns its keep is flagged in plain words.

  ```python
  # -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  # THE PAINTED FACTS (handed to us, fixed)
  #   runway  -> +5      dirt -> -2      fuel in the air -> -0.1
  #   shrink (the discount) -> 0.9
  # -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  RUNWAY = 5.0
  DIRT   = -2.0
  FUEL   = -0.1
  SHRINK = 0.9

  # =======================================================================
  # CASE 1: THE STACK ABOVE THE RUNWAY -- watch the lie HEAL, walk by walk.
  # Three spots, arrows point DOWN, finger runs TOP to BOTTOM (T, M, G).
  # That order forces the stale read: T and M are written before what they
  # lean on, so walk 1 reads junk -- and it still heals by walk 3.
  # =======================================================================
  print("=== CASE 1: the lie heals (top-down, forced stale reads) ===")

  # start: the deliberate lie -- EVERYTHING at 0, even the ground (not written yet this walk).
  T, M, G = 0.0, 0.0, 0.0

  # WALK 1 -- finger runs T, then M, then G. T and M both read a stale 0.
  T = FUEL + SHRINK * M    # reads M=0 (stale)  -> -0.1 + 0.9*0 = -0.1
  M = FUEL + SHRINK * G    # reads G=0 (stale!) -> -0.1 + 0.9*0 = -0.1
  G = RUNWAY               # ground written LAST -> +5  (the anchor lands, true forever now)
  print(f"walk 1:  T={T:.4f}   M={M:.4f}   G={G:.1f}")
  # walk 1:  T=-0.1000   M=-0.1000   G=5.0
  # both air spots leaned on stale 0s, so both are junk. Only the anchor G is true.

  # WALK 2 -- M now reads the true anchor; M snaps true. T still trails.
  T = FUEL + SHRINK * M    # reads M=-0.1  -> -0.19
  M = FUEL + SHRINK * G    # reads G=5     ->  4.4   (M is now TRUE)
  G = RUNWAY
  print(f"walk 2:  T={T:.4f}   M={M:.4f}   G={G:.1f}")
  # walk 2:  T=-0.1900   M=4.4000   G=5.0

  # WALK 3 -- T now reads the true M; T snaps true.
  T = FUEL + SHRINK * M    # reads M=4.4 -> 3.86   (T is now TRUE)
  M = FUEL + SHRINK * G    # 4.4 (no change)
  G = RUNWAY
  print(f"walk 3:  T={T:.4f}   M={M:.4f}   G={G:.1f}")
  # walk 3:  T=3.8600   M=4.4000   G=5.0

  # WALK 4 -- nothing moves: settled.
  print("walk 4:  no move -> STOP")
  print("the anchor +5 climbed one spot per walk: G(walk1) -> M(walk2) -> T(walk3).")

  # =======================================================================
  # CASE 2: A FOREVER-BOUNCE -- no ground at all. A and B point at each other.
  # The drone never lands. Show it STILL settles, to -1, by hand.
  # =======================================================================
  print("\n=== CASE 2: forever-bounce A <-> B (no anchor) settles to -1 ===")

  A, B = 0.0, 0.0   # both start at the lie

  A = FUEL + SHRINK * B    # -0.1 + 0.9*0       = -0.1
  B = FUEL + SHRINK * A    # -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.1)  = -0.19
  print(f"walk 1:  A={A:.4f}   B={B:.4f}")
  # walk 1:  A=-0.1000   B=-0.1900

  A = FUEL + SHRINK * B    # -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.19)   = -0.271
  B = FUEL + SHRINK * A    # -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.271)  = -0.3439
  print(f"walk 2:  A={A:.4f}   B={B:.4f}")
  # walk 2:  A=-0.2710   B=-0.3439

  A = FUEL + SHRINK * B    # -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.3439) = -0.40951
  print(f"walk 3:  A={A:.4f}   (crawling toward -1)")
  # walk 3:  A=-0.4095   (crawling toward -1)

  # The closed form, proved in the text: x = -0.1 / (1 - 0.9) = -1.
  closed_form = FUEL / (1 - SHRINK)   # -0.1 / 0.1 = -1.0
  print(f"closed form: -0.1 / (1 - 0.9) = {closed_form:.1f}  <- where the bounce lands")
  # closed form: -0.1 / (1 - 0.9) = -1.0
  print("drop the shrink (use 1.0): 1+1+1+... = infinity -> worth = -inf -> NEVER settles.")
  print("so the 0.9 is the floor that makes an answer EXIST -- not a 'care less' knob.")

  # =======================================================================
  # CASE 3: THE REAL ENGINE -- the whole law, laid down again and again.
  # WHY a loop here (and not hard-coded like above): the engine's POINT is
  # the repetition itself -- 'do the walk again.' Hard-coding it would hide
  # the one idea we are teaching. The TWO lines inside are the same law you
  # just did by hand; the loop only repeats them. That is the honest place
  # an abstraction belongs: it is the lesson, not a hiding spot.
  # =======================================================================
  print("\n=== CASE 3: the real engine on the runway stack ===")

  # the plan sends each air spot to the spot below it (always Down).
  # spots: 'G' is the runway (ground), 'M' one up, 'T' two up. next[spot]=where its arrow lands.
  is_ground = {'G': True,  'M': False, 'T': False}
  painted   = {'G': RUNWAY, 'M': None,  'T': None}   # only ground spots are painted
  goes_to   = {'M': 'G',   'T': 'M'}                 # the plan's arrows

  worth = {'G': 0.0, 'M': 0.0, 'T': 0.0}   # the lie
  theta = 1e-8

  for walk in range(1, 1000):              # 'do the walk again and again' -- the seatbelt cap
      delta = 0.0
      for spot in ['T', 'M', 'G']:         # one walk down the list, top to bottom (G written last)
          old = worth[spot]
          if is_ground[spot]:
              worth[spot] = painted[spot]                       # anchor: painted, no lookup
          else:
              worth[spot] = FUEL + SHRINK * worth[goes_to[spot]]  # -0.1 + 0.9*worth(next)
          delta = max(delta, abs(worth[spot] - old))            # biggest move this walk
      if delta < theta:                    # nothing moved more than a hair -> settled
          print(f"settled after {walk} walks:  T={worth['T']:.4f}  M={worth['M']:.4f}  G={worth['G']:.1f}")
          break
  # settled after 4 walks:  T=3.8600  M=4.4000  G=5.0
  ```

  Output of the three blocks:

      === CASE 1: the lie heals (top-down, forced stale reads) ===
      walk 1:  T=-0.1000   M=-0.1000   G=5.0
      walk 2:  T=-0.1900   M=4.4000   G=5.0
      walk 3:  T=3.8600   M=4.4000   G=5.0
      walk 4:  no move -> STOP
      the anchor +5 climbed one spot per walk: G(walk1) -> M(walk2) -> T(walk3).

      === CASE 2: forever-bounce A <-> B (no anchor) settles to -1 ===
      walk 1:  A=-0.1000   B=-0.1900
      walk 2:  A=-0.2710   B=-0.3439
      walk 3:  A=-0.4095   (crawling toward -1)
      closed form: -0.1 / (1 - 0.9) = -1.0  <- where the bounce lands
      drop the shrink (use 1.0): 1+1+1+... = infinity -> worth = -inf -> NEVER settles.
      so the 0.9 is the floor that makes an answer EXIST -- not a 'care less' knob.

      === CASE 3: the real engine on the runway stack ===
      settled after 4 walks:  T=3.8600  M=4.4000  G=5.0

  > THE ONE HONEST FOOTNOTE (named again, loudly). The air law here reads ONE next-spot:
  > `FUEL + 0.9 * worth(next)`. Real policy evaluation lets the arrow land on several next-spots
  > by chance and adds them up weighted by their odds:
  > `worth = reward + 0.9 * sum over next of  P(next) * worth(next)`.
  > Our plan is deterministic -- the arrow lands on exactly one spot -- so P(next) = 1 for that
  > one spot and 0 for the rest, and the whole sum collapses to the single term you saw. That is
  > the only thing the toy hides. Add the weighted sum back and every walk, every heal, every
  > stop rule on this page works unchanged.
