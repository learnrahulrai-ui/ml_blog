  Every reinforcement-learning post hands you the Bellman equation -- V(s) = R(s) + g * V(next)
  -- names it after a dead mathematician, and says "now just iterate until it converges." Two
  things most posts skip. First: WHY starting every value at 0 -- a flat lie -- ever crawls to
  the right answer. That smells like astrology: begin wrong, end right, no reason given. Second:
  WHY the discount g (the 0.9) is not a "care less about tomorrow" mood knob but, for a flight
  that might never land, the thing that keeps the point-total finite at all. (If every flight is
  guaranteed to land in a bounded number of moves, you do not strictly need it -- more on that
  caveat when we hit it.) This page shows both, digit by digit, with a pencil. The formula
  appears at the end, after you have already used it.

  TOY WORLD, REAL RECIPE. One line of honesty up front: start at a lie, lay the same
  two-line rule down again and again, watch it settle -- exact. The WORLD is a toy:
  a tiny stack of spots, simple painted scores, a plan with one fixed arrow per spot. Real
  policy evaluation lets the arrow land on several next-spots by chance and adds them up
  weighted by their odds; here the plan is fixed and the arrow lands on exactly ONE next-spot,
  so that weighted sum collapses to a single term. That collapse is the toy. It is named again,
  loudly, in the box at the end -- so the one place we hide a sum behind a single arrow is never
  smuggled past you. Trust the recipe; treat the rest as a sketchpad.

  KEY: THE HALL OF MIRRORS TRAP -- why you cannot just calculate worths directly
     Spot A's worth depends on spot B's worth. Spot B's worth depends on spot A's worth.
     Worth(A) = reward + Worth(B).  Worth(B) = reward + Worth(A).
     The equations loop back on each other. You cannot solve them in a straight line.
     THE FIX: lie first. Set every worth to 0. Sweep across all spots, updating each
     one from its neighbours using the formula. Sweep again. The numbers settle toward
     the true values -- the same truth the loop equation would give if you could solve it.
     The lie heals itself, one sweep at a time.

  The one move, named here so you are not surprised later:

      A worth leans on its neighbour's worth, which leans on ITS neighbour's worth -- a circle.
      You cannot write a bare number for any spot up front. So you LIE: write 0 everywhere,
      then lay the rule down over the table again and again. The lie heals. The truth climbs
      out from one painted anchor, a spot at a time, until nothing moves.

  That single trick turns a circular formula into plain arithmetic you can check by hand.

  THE WORLD (THE PICTURE EVERYTHING LIVES IN)

  A drone hangs in the air over a patch of enemy ground. We want to grade a flight PLAN.

      a SPOT      = a place the drone can be: a position with a height.
      the GROUND  = the lowest spots. Touching the ground ENDS the flight.
      the RUNWAY  = one special ground spot. Touch it -> +5 points. (the prize)
      DIRT        = any other ground spot. Touch it -> -2 points. (a crash)
      the AIR     = any spot above the ground. The drone does not stop here; it must move on.
      FUEL        = sitting in the air for one move costs -0.1 points. (the meter always runs)
      the ARROW   = the PLAN. On every air spot, an arrow says "from here, go THIS way."
      the SHRINK  = a worth one move into the future counts as 0.9 of itself. (the famous "g")

  These are PAINTED FACTS. +5, -2, -0.1, 0.9 -- handed to you, fixed, never argued with.

  THE WORLD, DRAWN (3 wide x 3 tall; the plan arrows all point DOWN):

      height 2  [ air ]  [ air ]  [ air ]    <- drone starts here, pays fuel each move
                   |        |        |
                   v        v        v           (arrows = the fixed plan we are grading)
      height 1  [ air ]  [ air ]  [ air ]
                   |        |        |
                   v        v        v
      height 0  [DIRT]  [RUNWY]  [DIRT]     <- ground: touching ends the flight
                  -2      +5      -2

      (9 columns total in the full 3x3 grid; the runway is the centre ground spot)

  A SINGLE STACK (the column directly above the runway, used as the worked example):

            height 2:  T  (top air spot)      <- arrow points down to M
                       |
                       v
            height 1:  M  (middle air spot)   <- arrow points down to G
                       |
                       v
            height 0:  G  (ground = runway)   = +5  (painted, no lookup)

  Jargon parked here, to be ignored until the decoder at the end: a spot = a "state". The plan
  = the "policy". A spot's worth = its "value" V(s). Painted points = the "reward". The shrink
  = the "discount factor", written gamma. Touching the ground = a "terminal state".

  WHAT "GRADE THE PLAN" EVEN MEANS -- THE WORTH OF A SPOT

  Someone hands you the PLAN (the arrows). You are NOT asked to improve it. You are asked one
  thing, for every spot: what is this spot's WORTH?

  Worth is NOT the raw physical points the drone walks away with. It is the DISCOUNTED sum: a
  reward collected k moves from now counts as 0.9^k of itself -- shrunk once per move into the
  future. So a +5 collected two moves away counts as 0.9^2 * 5 = 4.05, not 5.

  DISCOUNTING ILLUSTRATED -- the same +5 seen from different distances:

      distance   shrink factor   discounted value
         0       0.9^0 = 1.00    1.00 * 5 = 5.00   (you are standing on the runway)
         1       0.9^1 = 0.90    0.90 * 5 = 4.50   (one move away)
         2       0.9^2 = 0.81    0.81 * 5 = 4.05   (two moves away = spot T)
         3       0.9^3 = 0.73    0.73 * 5 = 3.65   (three moves away)

  The drone physically walks away with -0.1 - 0.1 + 5 = 4.8 raw points from spot T.
  The discounted WORTH at T is 3.86. The gap is the discount eating into future rewards.

  Work it out for the runway stack (T at top, M middle, G ground, arrows pointing DOWN):

      worth(T)  =  -0.1              (fuel, NOW, shrunk 0.9^0 = 1)
                +  0.9 * (-0.1)      (fuel at M, one move away, shrunk once)
                +  0.9^2 * (+5)      (runway at G, two moves away, shrunk twice)
               =  -0.1 - 0.09 + 4.05  =  3.86

  The drone physically walks away with  -0.1 - 0.1 + 5 = 4.8 raw points. The worth is 3.86
  discounted points. The gap (4.8 - 3.86 = 0.94) is not points lost in the world; it is the
  discount eating into future rewards in the METRIC we chose. Worth measures how good a spot is
  by the discounted yardstick; that yardstick is what makes the circular Bellman equation below
  consistent. Fill in the worth of every spot and you have graded the plan -- a good plan scores
  high worths, a bad plan scores low ones. That is the whole job. Nothing here invents arrows or
  picks a better path. It only SCORES the arrows you were given, on the discounted scale.

  ONE LAW (DERIVED, NOT DECLARED)

  A spot's worth = the DISCOUNTED points the drone collects from here on, obeying the arrows.
  Where the spot sits decides the shape.

  A ground spot. Touching the ground ends the flight. Nothing comes after. There is no
  future to count -- the worth is just the points painted there:

      worth(ground)  =  its painted points          (+5 on the runway, -2 on dirt)

  No lookup, no neighbour, no arithmetic. The number is painted on. Burn that in: a ground
  spot's worth is TRUE the instant you write it down, and can never be wrong.

  An air spot. The drone does not stop; it pays fuel and rides the arrow onward. Two parts:

      it pays fuel right now ........................... -0.1
      one move along the arrow sits the NEXT spot,
      whose worth counts 0.9 of itself (the shrink) ... 0.9 * worth(next)

  Add the two:

      worth(air)  =  -0.1  +  0.9 * worth(next)

  That is the entire law. Two lines. ('next' = the single spot the arrow points to.)

  WHY THERE IS NO ONE-SHOT FORMULA -- THE CIRCLE

  Look hard at the air line:

      worth(air)  =  -0.1  +  0.9 * worth(next)

  To get this spot's worth you need 'worth(next)'. But if 'next' is also an air spot, ITS worth
  needs the worth of ITS next, and on it goes. A worth leans on another worth, which leans on
  another. You cannot just write 'worth = <number>' for an air spot -- the number you need is
  one you do not have yet. That circle is the whole obstacle. Your gut is right to balk: there
  is no formula that spits out the answer in one shot WITH A PENCIL.

  (One honest aside, so a sharp reader is not misled: the circle is a set of linear equations,
  and a set of linear equations CAN be solved in one shot -- by matrix algebra, inverting
  (I - 0.9 * P). That is a real method, not pencil work, and it scales badly: a million spots is
  a million-by-million matrix. The walk below is what you actually do by hand and what scales,
  so that is what we build. "No one-shot formula" means none you run down a column with a
  pencil -- not none in all of mathematics.)

  BREAKING THE CIRCLE -- THE WALK, AND STARTING FROM A LIE

  New word, defined by a picture:

      A WALK = run your finger over the list of spots ONCE, in some fixed order, writing each
               spot's worth from the law above -- using whatever the table holds RIGHT NOW,
               even if that neighbour's worth is still a lie.

  (Picture a shopping list: one finger-trip over it = one walk. Over it again = a second walk.)
  The ORDER is a free choice -- top-to-bottom, bottom-to-top, any fixed sweep. We use top-to-
  bottom in the heal trace just below because it FORCES the worst case (stale reads) so you can
  watch the lie heal slowly; later, for the full grid, we flip to bottom-to-top because it is
  faster. Same answer either way; only the walk-count changes. (Why it changes is the next note.)

  One choice hiding in "whatever the table holds RIGHT NOW," named so it is not smuggled: when
  we write a spot we read its neighbour's value AS FRESH AS THE TABLE HOLDS -- including a value
  written EARLIER THIS SAME WALK, not only last walk's. Reading in place like this has a name,
  Gauss-Seidel; reading only last walk's frozen copy is the other name, Jacobi. Both settle to
  the same answer. We use Gauss-Seidel, and it is exactly why -- with the right walk direction --
  the truth can jump a whole spot per walk instead of crawling. So "one spot per walk" below is
  a property of THIS reading order, not a law of the algorithm: walk the stack the other way and
  the same truth would take more walks to climb.

  The recipe in full:

      set every spot's worth to 0          <- a deliberate lie, just to have a starting table
      do a walk. do another. do another.
      stop when a walk moves nothing.

  That is it. There is NO line that checks "is the neighbour ready," no junk-detector. The only
  thing ever measured is "did this walk move anything," and that is the stop rule, nothing finer.
  Just the two-line law, laid down again and again.

  WHY STARTING FROM A LIE STILL LANDS ON THE TRUTH (THE HEART, WITH NUMBERS)

  Two facts make the lie heal. Neither is magic; both are arithmetic.

  The "+" lays down a SOLID layer. The air law is 'reward + 0.9 * next'. The reward part
  (the -0.1 fuel) is solid -- it is painted, it needs no lookup, it goes down on every walk no
  matter what the neighbour says. So even reading a junk neighbour (still 0):

      worth  =  -0.1  +  0.9 * 0  =  -0.1          <- NOT 0. A solid -0.1 got laid down.

  The next walk adds another -0.1 plus 0.9 times a now-firmer neighbour. The value BUILDS by
  addition. Junk cannot multiply into junk-forever, because a solid layer is added on top every
  pass. The "+" is the floor under the "*".

  The ground is a fixed ANCHOR. A ground spot's worth is its painted points, with no
  lookup -- correct from the first walk, never junk. That is the one fixed truth every air spot
  is dragged toward.

  Watch it heal. Three spots stacked straight above the runway, arrows pointing DOWN, finger
  running top to bottom (T first, then M, then G) -- the order that FORCES the stale read you
  were worried about, because T and M get written before the spot they lean on.

      T = top    (air),  arrow down -> M
      M = middle (air),  arrow down -> G
      G = ground = runway,  painted +5     (the anchor)

  Everything starts at 0.

      WALK 1   (start T=0, M=0, G=0)
        T:  -> M reads 0 (stale!)   worth = -0.1 + 0.9*0  = -0.1
        M:  -> G reads 0 (stale!)   worth = -0.1 + 0.9*0  = -0.1
        G:  ground -> +5                                          <- anchor lands. TRUE forever.
        table: T=-0.1   M=-0.1   G=+5

      WALK 2   (start T=-0.1, M=-0.1, G=+5)
        T:  -> M reads -0.1          worth = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.1) = -0.19
        M:  -> G reads +5 (true!)    worth = -0.1 + 0.9*5      =  4.4   <- M is now TRUE
        G:  +5
        table: T=-0.19   M=4.4   G=+5

      WALK 3   (start T=-0.19, M=4.4, G=+5)
        T:  -> M reads 4.4 (true!)   worth = -0.1 + 0.9*4.4 = 3.86      <- T is now TRUE
        M:  -> G reads +5            worth = 4.4   (no change)
        G:  +5
        table: T=3.86   M=4.4   G=+5

      WALK 4   nothing moves. 3.86, 4.4, +5 stay put. STOP.

  Follow the top spot heal:

      T:   0   ->   -0.1   ->   -0.19   ->   3.86   ->   3.86
          start    walk1      walk2       walk3      walk4 (frozen)

  It started as junk read off a stale 0 and it healed. The reason, with no hand-waving:

      G (the anchor) is correct from walk 1.
      M leans on G  -> M becomes correct one walk later (walk 2).
      T leans on M  -> T becomes correct one walk after that (walk 3).

  The truth climbs ONE spot per walk, out from the anchor -- because we read in place (Gauss-
  Seidel) AND walk top-to-bottom so each spot is written just after the one it leans on. Walk
  bottom-to-top instead and the climb is slower; the "one spot per walk" is a gift of this
  order, not a law. The painted +5 cannot lie, and the rule drags the next spot to it, then the
  next. As tall as the stack is, that is how many walks the truth needs to reach the top. Then
  it freezes.

  > We do not start from a lie and hope. We start from a lie sitting next to ONE painted truth,
  > and plain multiplication drags every spot to that truth, one spot closer per pass.

  HARDER CASE: A FOREVER-BOUNCE WITH NO GROUND AT ALL

  "Fine," you say, "it heals because there is a +5 anchor to climb to. What if the drone never
  lands?" Good -- that is the case that looks impossible.

  BOUNCE, DRAWN:

         spot A  <----+
            |         |
            |  arrow  |  arrow
            v         |
         spot B  -----+

  Two air spots, A and B, arrows pointing at each OTHER forever. The drone bounces A, B, A,
  B... and never touches ground. No anchor anywhere. Both start at 0.

      WALK 1   A = -0.1 + 0.9*(0)        = -0.1
               B = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.1)     = -0.19
      WALK 2   A = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.19)    = -0.271
               B = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.271)   = -0.3439
      WALK 3   A = -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.3439)  = -0.40951
        ...    -0.1 -> -0.271 -> -0.41 -> -0.52 -> ... -> -1

  It crawls to -1 and holds. No ground was ever touched, yet it settled on a real number. How
  can an endless bounce cost a finite amount? Because of the shrink.

  WHY THE SHRINK FORCES A FINITE ANSWER (THE MATH CRACKED FROM SCRATCH)

  Bounce forever, paying -0.1 a move, with one more shrink stacked on each move:

      total  =  -0.1  +  0.9*(-0.1)  +  0.9^2*(-0.1)  +  0.9^3*(-0.1)  +  ...
             =  -0.1 * ( 1 + 0.9 + 0.9^2 + 0.9^3 + ... )

  Call the bracket S. Derive S from nothing -- no formula recalled, built right here:

      S        =  1 + 0.9 + 0.9^2 + 0.9^3 + ...
      0.9 * S  =      0.9 + 0.9^2 + 0.9^3 + ...
      S - 0.9S =  1                          (every later term cancels, one against the next)
      S(1 - 0.9) = 1
      S = 1 / (1 - 0.9) = 1 / 0.1 = 10

  So total = -0.1 * 10 = -1. Infinite moves, FINITE total -- the far terms shrink to nothing.
  Cross-check by the circle itself: with A = B = x, the law says x = -0.1 + 0.9x, so 0.1x = -0.1,
  so x = -1. Two routes, same -1.

  Now the punchline. First, the fence: the drone world is episodic -- every path lands eventually,
  so even with shrink = 1.0 the sum is finite and the math works (T would be 4.8, M 4.9, G 5).
  The bounce is different. The bounce is a CONTINUING task -- no ground exists, the flight never
  ends -- and that is the case where the shrink earns its keep.

  Drop the shrink from the bounce -- use a plain 1 instead of 0.9. The bracket becomes
  1 + 1 + 1 + ... = infinity. The worth is -infinity. The walks NEVER settle. So for any task
  that might run forever, 0.9 < 1 is not a mood ("value the future a little less"). It is the
  floor that forbids an infinite sum from blowing up. Almost every real control problem is a
  continuing task -- the robot does not stop, the server does not close. That is why the shrink
  is in the standard formulation: not as a taste-knob but as the thing that keeps totals finite
  when termination is not guaranteed.

  WHEN TO STOP -- DELTA AND THETA

  Two more words, defined:

      delta = the biggest move any spot made on a walk = the largest (new minus old), sign dropped.
      theta = a tiny line, e.g. 0.00000001 -- "near enough."

      stop rule:  when delta < theta, no spot moved more than a hair this walk -> the worths are
                  within a hair of obeying the law -> STOP.

  Say what delta < theta does and does NOT mean, exactly. It does NOT mean the table obeys the
  law perfectly -- you can sit a hair below the true value on every spot and still pass. It
  means the LAST walk barely nudged anything, and here is why that bounds your error: in the
  worst sweep ordering, each walk multiplies whatever error remains by at most 0.9 (the same
  shrink). In a better ordering -- bottom-to-top when arrows point down -- the error can vanish
  in a single fill-walk (Case 4 proves it: 27 spots, settled after 1 pass). Either way, 0.9 is
  a ceiling: the error cannot grow, only shrink. Pick theta small enough and "barely moved" and
  "basically correct" become the same thing for any use you have. That geometric guarantee -- the
  0.9 as a ceiling on error growth -- is the whole reason the walks land at all; it has a fancy
  name (a contraction mapping), but the 0.9 is all of it.

  A safety cap on the number of walks is just a seatbelt so a bug cannot loop forever. The real
  stop is delta < theta.

  ONLY CHECK IS "DID ANYTHING MOVE" -- THE SETTLING IS EMERGENT

  This is the part that feels too small to work. There is no code that spots junk, waits for a
  neighbour, or certifies a single spot is "correct." There is exactly ONE measurement in the
  whole engine -- delta, the biggest move this walk -- and it is used only to decide when to
  stop, never to fix or gate a value. The healing above and the finite bounce are CONSEQUENCES
  of repeating a "+ and *0.9" rule -- nowhere is either one coded. The code looks too simple to
  deserve the result, and that is correct: the work lives in the REPETITION and the 0.9 (the
  geometric squeeze of the last section), not in any clever check.

  A FULL TABLE, FILLED BY HAND -- AND WHAT IT REVEALS

  One more pass, the whole picture, so the payoff is concrete. A 3-wide, 3-tall world: spots
  are (x, y, height), each of x, y, height in {0,1,2}. The runway is the middle ground spot
  (1,1,0) -> +5. Every other ground spot is dirt -> -2. The plan: always nudge DOWN. So an air
  spot (x, y, height) leans on the spot one below it, (x, y, height-1). Walk each stack bottom
  to top -- the FAST order for down-arrows, since the spot below is already written this same
  walk (the opposite of Case 1 below, where we walked top-to-bottom on purpose to drag the heal
  out slowly so you could watch it). Same answer either way; only the number of walks differs.

  The stack ABOVE the runway -- sitting (1,1,0)=+5, (1,1,1), (1,1,2):

      (1,1,0)  ground, the runway  -> +5
      (1,1,1)  -0.1 + 0.9*( +5 )   =  4.4
      (1,1,2)  -0.1 + 0.9*( 4.4 )  =  3.86

  ANY OTHER stack -- sitting on dirt (?, ?, 0) = -2:

      (x,y,0)  ground, dirt        -> -2
      (x,y,1)  -0.1 + 0.9*( -2 )   = -1.9
      (x,y,2)  -0.1 + 0.9*( -1.9 ) = -1.81

  The finished table, read at a glance:

      runway stack (1,1,*):     +5 ,  4.4 ,  3.86      <- sitting over the runway pays off
      every other stack:        -2 , -1.9 , -1.81      <- this plan dives straight into dirt

  The grade is in the numbers. "Always Down" is brilliant in exactly ONE stack -- the column
  over the runway -- and a disaster in the other eight, because everywhere else, Down means
  crash into bare dirt (-2). That worth table is the scorecard a NEXT step would read to re-aim
  the arrows (head toward the runway stack first, THEN drop) -- but re-aiming is a different
  job. Grading the plan we were handed is done.

  DECODER (JARGON -> PLAIN, MASTER SHEET)

      PEACOCK WORD                         WHAT IT ACTUALLY MEANS
      --------------------------------------   -------------------------------------------------
      state / s                                a spot -- a place the drone can be
      policy / pi                              the plan -- one arrow per spot, handed to you, fixed
      value / V(s)                             the spot's worth -- DISCOUNTED points from here on
                                               (not the raw sum; future rewards shrunk by 0.9^k)
      reward / R(s)                            the painted points at a spot (+5 / -2 / -0.1)
      terminal state                           a ground spot -- touching it ends the flight
      discount factor / gamma                  the shrink (0.9) -- a future worth counts 0.9 per move
      transition / result(s, a)                where the arrow lands you next
      Bellman equation                         the one law; here in its deterministic form
                                               worth = reward + shrink * worth(next). General
                                               form sums over chance next-spots (see footnote)
      policy evaluation                        the whole job: fill every spot's worth for a fixed plan
      iterative policy evaluation              do it by walks -- start at 0, lay the law down, repeat
      sweep / iteration                        one walk down the list of spots
      convergence                              the walks stopped moving -- delta fell below theta
      delta                                    biggest worth-move on a walk
      theta                                    the "near enough" line that ends the walks
      bootstrapping (RL sense)                 writing a worth from another worth-ESTIMATE (the
                                               table's current number) instead of from a finished
                                               full count of real points -- here, leaning on the
                                               neighbour's table entry, settled or not

  PYTHON: THE SETTLING, BY HAND, FOUR WAYS

  Four blocks. The first two are hard-coded spot by spot -- no loops hiding the decisions, so
  you can check every digit against the walks above. The third is the real engine on the tiny
  stack. The fourth runs that SAME engine over the whole 27-spot grid, so the skeptic who wants
  more than three spots can watch the by-hand table fall out of real code. Every loop that
  appears is flagged in plain words: it lists positions or repeats the law -- it never hides a
  decision.

  # -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  # THE PAINTED FACTS (handed to us, fixed)
  #   runway  -> +5      dirt -> -2      fuel in the air -> -0.1
  #   shrink (the discount) -> 0.9
  # -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  RUNWAY = 5.0
  DIRT   = -2.0
  FUEL   = -0.1
  SHRINK = 0.9

  # =======================================================================
  # CASE 1: THE STACK ABOVE THE RUNWAY -- watch the lie HEAL, walk by walk.
  # Three spots, arrows point DOWN, finger runs TOP to BOTTOM (T, M, G).
  # That order forces the stale read: T and M are written before what they
  # lean on, so walk 1 reads junk -- and it still heals by walk 3.
  # =======================================================================
  print("=== CASE 1: the lie heals (top-down, forced stale reads) ===")

  # start: the deliberate lie -- EVERYTHING at 0, even the ground (not written yet this walk).
  T, M, G = 0.0, 0.0, 0.0

  # WALK 1 -- finger runs T, then M, then G. T and M both read a stale 0.
  T = FUEL + SHRINK * M    # reads M=0 (stale)  -> -0.1 + 0.9*0 = -0.1
  M = FUEL + SHRINK * G    # reads G=0 (stale!) -> -0.1 + 0.9*0 = -0.1
  G = RUNWAY               # ground written LAST -> +5  (the anchor lands, true forever now)
  print(f"walk 1:  T={T:.4f}   M={M:.4f}   G={G:.1f}")
  # walk 1:  T=-0.1000   M=-0.1000   G=5.0
  # both air spots leaned on stale 0s, so both are junk. Only the anchor G is true.

  # WALK 2 -- M now reads the true anchor; M snaps true. T still trails.
  T = FUEL + SHRINK * M    # reads M=-0.1  -> -0.19
  M = FUEL + SHRINK * G    # reads G=5     ->  4.4   (M is now TRUE)
  G = RUNWAY
  print(f"walk 2:  T={T:.4f}   M={M:.4f}   G={G:.1f}")
  # walk 2:  T=-0.1900   M=4.4000   G=5.0

  # WALK 3 -- T now reads the true M; T snaps true.
  T = FUEL + SHRINK * M    # reads M=4.4 -> 3.86   (T is now TRUE)
  M = FUEL + SHRINK * G    # 4.4 (no change)
  G = RUNWAY
  print(f"walk 3:  T={T:.4f}   M={M:.4f}   G={G:.1f}")
  # walk 3:  T=3.8600   M=4.4000   G=5.0

  # WALK 4 -- nothing moves: settled.
  print("walk 4:  no move -> STOP")
  print("the anchor +5 climbed one spot per walk: G(walk1) -> M(walk2) -> T(walk3).")

  # =======================================================================
  # CASE 2: A FOREVER-BOUNCE -- no ground at all. A and B point at each other.
  # The drone never lands. Show it STILL settles, to -1, by hand.
  # =======================================================================
  print("\n=== CASE 2: forever-bounce A <-> B (no anchor) settles to -1 ===")

  A, B = 0.0, 0.0   # both start at the lie

  # Gauss-Seidel again: B reads the A just written this same walk, not the frozen old one.
  # Reproduce with frozen copies (Jacobi) and you get different intermediate numbers.
  A = FUEL + SHRINK * B    # -0.1 + 0.9*0       = -0.1
  B = FUEL + SHRINK * A    # -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.1)  = -0.19   (reads the NEW A=-0.1)
  print(f"walk 1:  A={A:.4f}   B={B:.4f}")
  # walk 1:  A=-0.1000   B=-0.1900

  A = FUEL + SHRINK * B    # -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.19)   = -0.271
  B = FUEL + SHRINK * A    # -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.271)  = -0.3439
  print(f"walk 2:  A={A:.4f}   B={B:.4f}")
  # walk 2:  A=-0.2710   B=-0.3439

  A = FUEL + SHRINK * B    # -0.1 + 0.9*(-0.3439) = -0.40951
  print(f"walk 3:  A={A:.4f}   (crawling toward -1)")
  # walk 3:  A=-0.4095   (crawling toward -1)

  # The closed form, proved in the text: x = -0.1 / (1 - 0.9) = -1.
  closed_form = FUEL / (1 - SHRINK)   # -0.1 / 0.1 = -1.0
  print(f"closed form: -0.1 / (1 - 0.9) = {closed_form:.1f}  <- where the bounce lands")
  # closed form: -0.1 / (1 - 0.9) = -1.0
  print("drop the shrink (use 1.0): 1+1+1+... = infinity -> worth = -inf -> NEVER settles.")
  print("so the 0.9 is the floor that makes an answer EXIST -- not a 'care less' knob.")

  # =======================================================================
  # CASE 3: THE REAL ENGINE -- the whole law, laid down again and again.
  # WHY a loop here (and not hard-coded like above): the engine's POINT is
  # the repetition itself -- 'do the walk again.' Hard-coding it would hide
  # the one idea we are teaching. The TWO lines inside are the same law you
  # just did by hand; the loop only repeats them. That is the honest place
  # an abstraction belongs: it is the lesson, not a hiding spot.
  # =======================================================================
  print("\n=== CASE 3: the real engine on the runway stack ===")

  # the plan sends each air spot to the spot below it (always Down).
  # spots: 'G' is the runway (ground), 'M' one up, 'T' two up. next[spot]=where its arrow lands.
  is_ground = {'G': True,  'M': False, 'T': False}
  painted   = {'G': RUNWAY, 'M': None,  'T': None}   # only ground spots are painted
  goes_to   = {'M': 'G',   'T': 'M'}                 # the plan's arrows

  worth = {'G': 0.0, 'M': 0.0, 'T': 0.0}   # the lie
  theta = 1e-8

  for walk in range(1, 1000):              # 'do the walk again and again' -- the seatbelt cap
      delta = 0.0
      for spot in ['T', 'M', 'G']:         # one walk down the list, top to bottom (G written last)
          old = worth[spot]
          if is_ground[spot]:
              worth[spot] = painted[spot]                       # anchor: painted, no lookup
          else:
              worth[spot] = FUEL + SHRINK * worth[goes_to[spot]]  # -0.1 + 0.9*worth(next)
          delta = max(delta, abs(worth[spot] - old))            # biggest move this walk
      if delta < theta:                    # nothing moved more than a hair -> settled
          print(f"settled after {walk} walks:  T={worth['T']:.4f}  M={worth['M']:.4f}  G={worth['G']:.1f}")
          break
  # settled after 4 walks:  T=3.8600  M=4.4000  G=5.0
  # (the VALUES reached truth on walk 3, same as the hand-trace; walk 4 is the confirming
  #  pass that moves nothing and trips delta < theta. the stop rule always costs one extra
  #  walk to NOTICE it has settled.)

  # =======================================================================
  # CASE 4: THE FULL 27-SPOT GRID -- same engine, no longer a toy stack.
  # The world is (x, y, height), each of x,y,height in {0,1,2} -> 27 spots.
  # Runway = (1,1,0) -> +5. Every other ground spot (height 0) -> dirt -2.
  # Plan = always nudge DOWN: an air spot (x,y,h) leans on (x,y,h-1).
  # We flip to BOTTOM-TO-TOP order here: write the ground first, then the
  # spot above it reads a value already true THIS walk -> the whole grid
  # settles in one fill-walk (plus one confirm walk to notice).
  # =======================================================================
  print("\n=== CASE 4: the full 27-spot grid ===")

  # The two LOOPS below only LIST positions -- they hide no decision. Listing
  # 27 coordinates by hand would be 27 identical lines; the loop is the honest
  # short-hand for "every (x,y,height)". The painted facts and the one law are
  # still hard-coded, exactly as in the cases above.
  heights = (0, 1, 2)
  cols    = [(x, y) for x in (0, 1, 2) for y in (0, 1, 2)]   # the 9 ground columns

  def painted_points(x, y):                 # ground only: runway vs dirt, hard-coded
      return RUNWAY if (x, y) == (1, 1) else DIRT

  worth = {}                                # the table, keyed by (x,y,height); the lie is "absent"
  for (x, y) in cols:
      for h in heights:
          worth[(x, y, h)] = 0.0            # start every spot at 0 -- the same deliberate lie

  theta = 1e-8
  for walk in range(1, 1000):
      delta = 0.0
      for h in heights:                     # BOTTOM-TO-TOP: ground (h=0) first, then up
          for (x, y) in cols:
              old = worth[(x, y, h)]
              if h == 0:
                  worth[(x, y, h)] = painted_points(x, y)        # ground: painted, no lookup
              else:
                  worth[(x, y, h)] = FUEL + SHRINK * worth[(x, y, h - 1)]  # the one law, down-arrow
              delta = max(delta, abs(worth[(x, y, h)] - old))
      if delta < theta:
          print(f"settled after {walk} walks")
          break

  # read three columns out of the finished table -- the runway column and two dirt columns.
  for (x, y) in [(1, 1), (0, 0), (2, 2)]:
      h0, h1, h2 = worth[(x,y,0)], worth[(x,y,1)], worth[(x,y,2)]
      tag = "RUNWAY column" if (x, y) == (1, 1) else "dirt column  "
      print(f"  {tag} ({x},{y},*):  {h0:.1f} , {h1:.4f} , {h2:.4f}")
  # settled after 2 walks
  #   RUNWAY column (1,1,*):  5.0 , 4.4000 , 3.8600   <- matches the by-hand table exactly
  #   dirt column   (0,0,*): -2.0 , -1.9000 , -1.8100
  #   dirt column   (2,2,*): -2.0 , -1.9000 , -1.8100

  Output of the four blocks:

      === CASE 1: the lie heals (top-down, forced stale reads) ===
      walk 1:  T=-0.1000   M=-0.1000   G=5.0
      walk 2:  T=-0.1900   M=4.4000   G=5.0
      walk 3:  T=3.8600   M=4.4000   G=5.0
      walk 4:  no move -> STOP
      the anchor +5 climbed one spot per walk: G(walk1) -> M(walk2) -> T(walk3).

      === CASE 2: forever-bounce A <-> B (no anchor) settles to -1 ===
      walk 1:  A=-0.1000   B=-0.1900
      walk 2:  A=-0.2710   B=-0.3439
      walk 3:  A=-0.4095   (crawling toward -1)
      closed form: -0.1 / (1 - 0.9) = -1.0  <- where the bounce lands
      drop the shrink (use 1.0): 1+1+1+... = infinity -> worth = -inf -> NEVER settles.
      so the 0.9 is the floor that makes an answer EXIST -- not a 'care less' knob.

      === CASE 3: the real engine on the runway stack ===
      settled after 4 walks:  T=3.8600  M=4.4000  G=5.0

      === CASE 4: the full 27-spot grid ===
      settled after 2 walks
        RUNWAY column (1,1,*):  5.0 , 4.4000 , 3.8600
        dirt column   (0,0,*): -2.0 , -1.9000 , -1.8100
        dirt column   (2,2,*): -2.0 , -1.9000 , -1.8100

  Note the walk-counts: Case 3 took 4 walks (top-to-bottom, the deliberately slow order that
  drags the heal out one spot at a time); Case 4 took 2 (bottom-to-top, ground written before
  the spot leaning on it, so the whole grid fills in a single pass plus one to notice). Same law,
  same answer -- only the sweep direction moved, and that is the whole of the difference.

  > THE ONE HONEST FOOTNOTE (named again, loudly). The air law here reads ONE next-spot:
  > 'FUEL + 0.9 * worth(next)'. Real policy evaluation lets the arrow land on several next-spots
  > by chance and adds them up weighted by their odds:
  > 'worth = reward + 0.9 * sum over next of  P(next) * worth(next)'.
  > Our plan is deterministic -- the arrow lands on exactly one spot -- so P(next) = 1 for that
  > one spot and 0 for the rest, and the whole sum collapses to the single term you saw. That is
  > the only thing the toy hides. Add the weighted sum back and every walk, every heal, every
  > stop rule on this page works unchanged.

  One Breath: a spot's worth is the DISCOUNTED sum of points collected from here obeying the
  arrows -- future rewards shrunk by 0.9 per move, so the drone's 4.8 raw points become T's
  worth of 3.86; the worth of an air spot leans on its neighbour's worth (a circle), so you
  start every spot at a lie of 0 and lay down '-0.1 + 0.9 * worth(next)' again and again -- the
  painted ground is a fixed anchor the truth climbs out from (how fast depends on sweep order,
  not on the algorithm); even a ground-less forever-bounce settles, to -1, because 0.9 < 1 turns
  the endless sum finite AND squeezes each walk's remaining error by the same factor, which is
  why the walks land even with no anchor -- not magic, just 0.9 doing two jobs at once; and the
  only thing the engine measures is whether the last walk moved anything -- that single delta is
  the stop rule, not a per-spot check.
